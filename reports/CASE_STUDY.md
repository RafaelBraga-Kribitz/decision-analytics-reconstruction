
# Case Study — Decision Analytics Reconstruction

Practitioner reconstruction of a national-scale campaign analytics stack: population
modeling (Module A), constrained resource allocation (Module B), and probabilistic
scenario analysis (Module C). **Canonical numbers:** see
[`NUMERIC_SSOT.md`](NUMERIC_SSOT.md).

---

## Business framing

**Setting.** A time-constrained campaign targeted a national eligible population
(**4,260,816** voters at full electoral-roll scale; **50,000** in the default
production reconstruction run) to influence a verifiable binary outcome on a fixed
date.

**Constraints.**

- **18-week** campaign scope; reconstruction pipeline models **weeks 1–14** (2018-W01–W14) where operational data exists
- **$6M USD** solver envelope (sole budget figure in portfolio narrative)
- 18 geographic units, 11 reach channels, 8 fixture tracking poll waves (+ 4 exit surveys)

**Verified outcome (TSJE, Series A):** **+3.70 pp** margin (46.43% vs 42.73%);
national turnout **61.25%**.

We do **not** claim a causal counterfactual ("analytics added X pp"). The
reconstruction demonstrates how segmentation, optimization, and forecasting
*could* support decisions under those constraints; the +3.70 pp anchor is the
public ground truth, not an A/B lift estimate.

---

## System 1 — Population segmentation (Module A)

Six operationally labeled segments from DBSCAN pre-pass + K-Means (k=6) on
synthetic features calibrated to TSJE/DGEEC marginals.

**Enforced CI gates:** silhouette **> 0.22** (measured 0.2562 at the 50k
production run, seed 42); bootstrap ARI **≥ 0.40** at 50k / **0.50** test floor
(measured 0.4304 at 50k). Legacy aspirational gates (0.35 / 0.80) and the retired
two-subsample ARI (~0.76) are superseded (IMP-A03/#55).

**Propensity model:** Platt-calibrated logistic regression with department raking
to turnout anchors. **Brier 0.1185** (15k holdout, seed 42; 0.1212 at the
50k production run) vs gate **< 0.237**. Reported **AUC ≈0.89 is circular** (target
shares calibration anchors with features) — see model card; do not headline AUC as
generalization.

---

## System 2 — Resource allocation (Module B)

MILP over **2,772** rows (18 × 11 × 14) maximizing linearized
persuasion-adjusted contacts under budget, reach-cap, FX corridor, and **80%**
coverage floor constraints. Solver status **OPTIMAL** on baseline scenario.

**Solver comparator (provable):** MILP vs department-uniform naive baseline yields
**~54.8%** more linearized persuasion-proxy contacts on the $6M envelope
(`run_manifest_baseline.json`; golden metric `linearized_lift_pct_milp_vs_naive`).
Framed as reconstruction-envelope optimization — not verified 2018 causal lift.

Department "calibration" in Module A is **post-hoc raking** to verified rates — it
validates aggregate alignment, not predictive skill on withheld microdata.

---

## System 3 — Probistic forecasting (Module C)

Bayesian hierarchical tracking (Gaussian random walk + pollster house effects) and
stratified Monte Carlo shock scenarios (**10,000** draws default). Posterior
margins on sparse fixtures are **wide and prior-dominated** — they illustrate
uncertainty machinery, not a tight election-eve point forecast. Always pair model
outputs with the verified **+3.70 pp** anchor.

**Diagnostics (honest):** the v0.4 non-centered reparameterization achieves **0
divergences** on the full NUTS run with R̂ ≤ 1.007 and ESS ≥ 1864, CI-enforced via
slow-lane tests (issue #60 / PR #73). Earlier centered-parameterization runs showed
divergences and R̂>1.01 on some parameters; v0.4 resolved them.

## A worked decision: week 8 replay

The three systems above are described in isolation. This section walks **one**
decision end-to-end: a week-8 poll posterior updates one strategic weight, the
Module B optimizer re-solves, and we read off the reallocation and its
uncertainty. Every number below is generated by
`scripts/generate_decision_replay.py` (deterministic, seed **20180422**) and
committed to `reports/decision_replay_results.json`, which this section cites.

**Honest wiring.** Module C is *downstream* of Module B (B→C, F-040), so the
pipeline does **not** feed the tracking posterior back into the allocator. This
is a disclosed **scenario replay**: we re-parameterize by hand the one solver
lever a campaign would update at mid-campaign. It is not an automatic pipeline
behavior.

**Decision context.** At plan time the allocator's swing-tier objective weight
sits at its committed value **1.10** — the emphasis placed on contested
departments. We identify that with the plan-time national lead read from the
Module C tracking posterior at the end of campaign week 1 (2018-01-07):
**+8.56 pp**.

**What changed at week 8.** By the end of week 8 (2018-02-25) the tracking
posterior put the national lead at **+17.65 pp**, 94% HDI **[+3.53, +30.80] pp** —
the lead had roughly doubled and the HDI no longer crossed zero. A campaign
seeing a wider, firmer lead pulls emphasis *off* the knife-edge swing
departments. The disclosed mapping is a single bounded rescale of the swing
weight:

> `swing_weight(m) = 1.10 × clip(m_plan / m, 1/3, 3)`, with `m_plan = 8.56 pp`.

At the week-8 mean (17.65 pp) this drops the swing weight to **0.533**; the MILP
is re-solved on the same **$6M** envelope. Solver status is **OPTIMAL** on the
baseline and all three replay solves.

**Reallocation** (baseline plan vs. week-8 mean replay; Δ contacts is the change
in raw reachable contacts, weight-independent so it is comparable across plans):

The decision moves **$766,285** off the six swing departments into strongholds
and opposition units. Top three shifts: **Central +$322,546**, **Alto Parana
−$255,023**, **Asuncion +$174,515**. Scored under the *week-8 belief* (the
decision-relevant weights), re-optimizing is worth **+1,609,974** persuasion-
adjusted contacts (**+1.20%**) over keeping the plan-time allocation; the
weight-independent physical cost is small (**−2.80M** raw contacts, ≈ −1.0%, as
budget concentrates into higher-diminishing-returns strongholds).

**What the uncertainty does to the decision.** Re-solving at the 94% HDI
endpoints shows the decision is not robust across the full posterior. At the
optimistic edge (**+30.80 pp**) the swing weight falls further to **0.367** and
the pull-off-swing decision only strengthens. But at the pessimistic edge
(**+3.53 pp** — a genuinely tight race) the mapping *reverses* the weight to
**2.670**, and budget flows the other way: Alto Parana's replayed spend ranges
from **$527,723** (HDI-high) to **$1,151,437** (HDI-low) versus its $783,849
baseline. In other words, the mean and the bulk of the HDI mass say "consolidate
the lead," but the lower tail says "double down on swing" — the sign of the
reallocation depends on where in the HDI the truth sits. That divergence is the
point: the decision is well-determined only if the campaign trusts the posterior
mean.

**Limitations.** The tracking posterior is an **illustrative fixture** (wide,
prior-dominated — see `epistemic_boundaries.md`), not an election-eve forecast.
The posterior→weight mapping is a **disclosed modeling choice** (inverse
proportionality, elasticity 1, bounded to a 3× band), on the same ESTIMATED
footing as the tier priors it rescales, and is **not** a measured causal
relationship: nothing here claims that reallocating budget *causes* a margin
change. The exercise demonstrates the *machinery* of updating a constrained
allocation from fresh evidence, with uncertainty carried through — not a
recommendation for the verified 2018 outcome (public anchor: **+3.70 pp**).

signals, single verifiable outcome. Scale numbers above are reconstruction-specific;
