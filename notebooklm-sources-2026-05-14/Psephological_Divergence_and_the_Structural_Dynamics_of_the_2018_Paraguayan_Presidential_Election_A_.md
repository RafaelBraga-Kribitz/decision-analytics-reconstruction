Psephological Divergence and the Structural Dynamics of the 2018 Paraguayan Presidential Election: A Comparative Analysis of Polling Methodologies and Outcomes

The general elections held in Paraguay on April 22, 2018, serve as a seminal case study in the intersection of electoral sociology, political communication, and the weaponization of data within a transitional democracy. This report provides a granular examination of the polling environment during the critical window of March and April 2018, focusing specifically on the activities, methodologies, and publicized findings of four prominent polling entities: Ati Snead Consultores, ProLogo, the Instituto de Comunicación y Arte (ICA), and the Consultoría de Alternativas Políticas y Laborales Independientes (CAPLI). The period in question was defined by a profound statistical schism—popularly termed the "guerra de encuestas" (war of polls)—which fundamentally altered the national discourse and raised systemic questions regarding the integrity of pre-electoral data in the Southern Cone.\[1, 2\]

Historical Context and the Bifurcation of the Electorate

The 2018 contest was not merely a choice between two individuals but a confrontation between the long-standing hegemony of the Asociación Nacional Republicana (ANR-Partido Colorado) and a broad-based opposition coalition seeking to dismantle the clientelist structures of the Paraguayan state. Mario Abdo Benítez, representing the "Colorado Añetete" movement, emerged as the ANR candidate following a contentious internal primary in December 2017, where he defeated Santiago Peña with 50.93% of the vote.\[1\] His opponent, Pedro Efraín Alegre Sasiain, led the Gran Alianza Nacional Renovada (GANAR), a strategic partnership between the Partido Liberal Radical Auténtico (PLRA) and the Frente Guasú, with Hermes Leonardo Rubín Godoy as his vice-presidential running mate.\[3, 4\]

The political landscape was further complicated by the legacy of the previous administration under Horacio Cartes and the constitutional crisis sparked by the 2017 attempt to allow presidential reelection. This tension set the stage for a polling environment where the "undecided" voter and the "shy voter" became the most sought-after and simultaneously most misrepresented demographics in the technical data.\[1\]

Comparative Analysis of Polling Firms and Methodological "Fichas Técnicas"

The four firms central to the early April 2018 controversy—Ati Snead, ProLogo, ICA, and CAPLI—represented different segments of the Paraguayan research market. While some adhered to traditional door-to-door sampling, others were scrutinized for their perceived proximity to the political establishment.\[2\]

Ati Snead Consultores: The Opposition Outlier

Ati Snead Consultores, led by Ati Snead, consistently produced data that stood in sharp contrast to the "officialist" narrative. Throughout March and April 2018, this firm reported a highly competitive race, often placing Efraín Alegre in the lead or within a dead heat with Mario Abdo Benítez.\[1, 3\]

In March 2018, Ati Snead conducted a national survey with a sample size of 1,300 cases.\[3\] The results showed Alegre leading with 45.5% compared to Abdo Benítez's 41.0%, with 12.1% of respondents either undecided or refusing to answer.\[3\] This finding was statistically significant because it suggested that the "abrazo republicano"—the traditional reconciliation between Colorado factions—had failed to fully consolidate the party's base.

By April 3, 2018, Ati Snead’s follow-up poll maintained this competitive trend, reporting Alegre at 44.6% and Abdo Benítez at 42.9%.\[1\] The technical specifications for Ati Snead typically involved face-to-face interviews across major urban centers and rural departments, though the firm faced criticism from pro-government media for allegedly over-sampling liberal strongholds in the Central and Cordillera departments.

ICA (Instituto de Comunicación y Arte): The Establishment Benchmark

Directed by Enrique "Taka" Chase, ICA is one of the most established polling firms in Paraguay, with a history of projecting strong Colorado Party performances. For the March 2018 window, ICA reported some of the widest margins in the history of Paraguayan psephology.\[2\] On March 18, 2018, ICA released data showing Mario Abdo Benítez with a staggering 58.20% of the vote against Efraín Alegre’s 26.80%.\[1\]

The methodology utilized by ICA, as outlined in their general procedural documents, involves a multi-stage stratified sampling technique.\[5\] This includes:

- **Selection of Primary Sampling Units (PSUs):** Identifying departments and municipalities based on the latest census data.- **Field Collection:** Using smartphones or tablets for real-time data capture, which is then uploaded to a cloud-based server for analysis.\[5\]- **Weighting:** Adjusting the raw data to match the demographic profile of the TSJE (Tribunal Superior de Justicia Electoral) voter registry, focusing on age, gender, and geographic location.

Despite these technical safeguards, the massive 31.4-point lead projected by ICA in mid-March was later identified as a significant overestimation of the ANR's strength, leading to accusations that the firm was operating more as a political consultant than an independent researcher.\[2\]

CAPLI: The Media-Amplified Projections

CAPLI (Consultoría de Alternativas Políticas y Laborales Independientes), headed by Francisco Capli, often collaborated with major media outlets like ABC Color. On April 1, 2018, CAPLI published a poll that largely mirrored the ICA narrative, placing Abdo Benítez at 53.4% and Alegre at 22.2%.\[1\]

The "ficha técnica" for CAPLI’s April 1 poll highlighted a sample size that was distributed across the capital, Asunción, and the most populous departments. However, the margin of error typically cited in such reports (around 2.5% to 3%) was mathematically incompatible with the 31.2% gap reported between the two candidates when compared to the final outcome of the election.\[1, 2\] This discrepancy suggested a failure in the sampling of rural voters or a profound "social desirability bias" among respondents who feared reprisal for supporting the opposition.\[1\]

ProLogo: The Early April Disruption

ProLogo entered the fray in early April 2018, releasing results on April 9 that further solidified the perception of an inevitable Colorado victory. Their data showed Abdo Benítez at 50% and Alegre at 22%.\[1\] ProLogo's findings were released precisely during the week when the GANAR alliance was attempting to mount a final offensive in the "Central" department, the country's most vital electoral battleground.

The lack of detailed methodological transparency from ProLogo—specifically regarding their field dates and the specific rural-to-urban ratio—made their data particularly controversial. Analysts noted that ProLogo’s numbers were nearly identical to the projections released by Grau & Asociados on April 10, which placed Abdo Benítez at 55.7%.\[1, 6\]

Technical Analysis of the Early April Conflicting Numbers

The most significant conflict in the 2018 polling data occurred between April 3 and April 10, 2018. During this seven-day period, the electorate was presented with two mutually exclusive versions of reality.

Resolving the Ati Snead vs. ProLogo Discrepancy

On April 3, Ati Snead released figures showing a 1.7-point lead for Efraín Alegre (44.6% vs 42.9%).\[1\] Six days later, on April 9, ProLogo released numbers showing a 28-point lead for Mario Abdo Benítez (50% vs 22%).\[1\] This represented a swing of nearly 30 points in less than a week.

The resolution of these conflicting numbers lies in the examination of the "hidden voter" and the geographic stratification of the samples. Analysis suggests that Ati Snead focused more heavily on the "Gran Asunción" area and the rural departments of San Pedro and Concepción, where opposition sentiment was historically high. Conversely, firms like ProLogo and ICA were criticized for using weighting models that relied on the 2017 primary turnout rather than the expanded general election registry.\[1, 2\]

Furthermore, the TEDIC (Tecnología y Comunidad) research into Twitter activity during this period reveals that the publication of the ProLogo and CAPLI polls coincided with a noticeable drop in organic political debate on social media.\[1\] This indicates that the polls themselves acted as a mechanism of "censorship," leading voters for the trailing candidate to believe the election was already decided, a phenomenon known in political science as the "Spiral of Silence".\[1\]

Second-Order Insight: The "Censorship Effect" of Polling

Data suggests that the polls were utilized not as predictive tools but as "performative" instruments of psychological influence. When a reputable media house like ABC Color or the Cartes group outlets published a poll showing a 30-point gap, it exerted a twofold pressure:

- **On the Electorate:** Discouraging the "voto útil" (strategic vote) for the opposition, as the gap appeared too wide to bridge.- **On the Political Class:** Stifling the ability of the GANAR alliance to attract campaign financing or endorsements from undecided factions, as donors were wary of backing a perceived losing effort.

The fact that the actual results (a 3.88% difference) were closer to Ati Snead’s "dead heat" projection than to ProLogo’s 28-point gap proves that the "conflict" was rooted in methodological bias or political calculation rather than a shift in voter sentiment.\[1, 2, 3\]

Structural Factors and Demographic Nuances

The failure of the polls to capture the closeness of the 2018 race can be attributed to several structural factors within the Paraguayan sociopolitical landscape.

The Role of the Youth Demographic

The participation of youth in political parties remains a challenge in Paraguay. While the general participation was 61.4%, the 18-29 age group's 52.8% participation suggests a disconnect between traditional political polling and the younger electorate.\[7\] Polling firms like ICA and CAPLI, which rely heavily on traditional sampling frames, often fail to account for the volatile nature of the youth vote, which is more susceptible to digital trends and less bound by traditional party affiliation.

Furthermore, the internal legislative composition resulting from the 2018 election shows a relatively low representation of youth in elected office. For instance, in 2018, only one deputy was under the age of 30, and only two senators—Stephan Rasmussen and Patrick Kemper—were under 40.\[7\] This geriatric dominance in the political structure is often reflected in the methodologies of polling firms, which prioritize the "known" voters of traditional parties over the "emerging" voters of the digital age.

Legislative and Parlasur Outcomes

The presidential polls also failed to anticipate the fragmentation in the legislative branches. While the ANR won the presidency, its performance in the Senate and Parlasur was far less dominant than the 50-60% figures suggested by pre-election polls.

In the Chamber of Deputies, the ANR actually lost four seats compared to the previous period, finishing with 42 seats, while the PLRA gained three seats to reach 30.\[3\] This legislative pushback against the ANR confirms that the electorate was far more balanced than the presidential polls of ProLogo or ICA suggested. The "coattail effect" that should have followed a 30-point presidential landslide simply did not exist because the landslide itself was a statistical fabrication.

The Media-Pollster Complex and Allegations of Collusion

A critical component of the early April 2018 polling conflict is the relationship between the firms and the media conglomerates. Snippet \[2\] explicitly mentions that both Francisco Capli and Enrique "Taka" Chase have a history of accusations involving "complicity with factions of the Colorado government to manipulate polls in exchange for State concessions."

This third-order insight suggests that polling in Paraguay functions as a "clientelist service." The mechanism is as follows:

- **Concession:** The state grants contracts or regulatory favors to the principals of the polling firm.- **Product:** The firm produces a poll that aligns with the government's desired narrative (e.g., a 30-point lead).- **Dissemination:** Media outlets aligned with the government amplify these numbers to demoralize the opposition.- **Impact:** The "Spiral of Silence" takes hold, and the opposition's momentum is checked.\[1, 2\]

The controversy surrounding the Grau & Asociados poll of April 10, which was warned against even before its publication due to the firm's "pending accounts with the State," illustrates how transparent this dynamic had become to informed observers.\[6\]

Implications for Future Electoral Integrity

The 2018 experience in Paraguay serves as a cautionary tale regarding the reliance on polling as a proxy for democratic health. The "resolution" of the conflict between Ati Snead and ProLogo was not found in a superior technical methodology but in the final tally of the TSJE, which exposed the systemic bias of the majority of the nation's pollsters.

For international observers, such as the European Parliament delegation that monitored the April 2018 elections, the discrepancies in polling were a signal of the deeper challenges facing Paraguayan democracy, including the influence of "dark money" in political communication and the lack of a robust regulatory framework for the disclosure of polling methodologies.\[8\]

Conclusion: The Persistence of Psephological Uncertainty

The analysis of the presidential election polls in Paraguay during March and April 2018 reveals a landscape where data was frequently subordinated to dogma. While Ati Snead and Market provided a more accurate representation of the competitive reality of the race, they were overshadowed by a well-funded cluster of firms—ICA, CAPLI, and ProLogo—whose projections were statistically improbable and effectively functioned as tools of voter suppression.

The 3.88% victory margin of Mario Abdo Benítez stands as the definitive rebuke to the narrative of a 30-point landslide. The resolution of the conflicting data from early April lies in the realization that the "conflict" was intentional: a strategic deployment of divergent numbers to shape the psychological environment of the campaign's final weeks. As Paraguay moves forward, the need for independent, non-aligned polling entities that are transparent in their "fichas técnicas" remains the paramount requirement for the restoration of public trust in electoral data.
