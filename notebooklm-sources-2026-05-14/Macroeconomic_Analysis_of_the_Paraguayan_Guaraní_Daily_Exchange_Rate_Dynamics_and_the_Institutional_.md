Macroeconomic Analysis of the Paraguayan Guaraní: Daily Exchange Rate Dynamics and the Institutional Framework of the Banco Central del Paraguay (January–April 2018)

The exchange rate between the United States Dollar (USD) and the Paraguayan Guaraní (PYG) is a fundamental pillar of the Paraguayan economy, serving as a critical indicator for monetary policy, international trade, and financial stability. Within the first four months of 2018, the Guaraní navigated a period of notable resilience and seasonal fluctuations that are emblematic of the Banco Central del Paraguay (BCP) and its managed flexibility regime. The "Cotización de Referencia," or the reference exchange rate, is the official benchmark published by the BCP, representing the weighted average of interbank and exchange house transactions conducted throughout a given business day.\[1, 2\] This report provides an exhaustive analysis of the daily dynamics, institutional mechanisms, and macroeconomic context that defined the USD/PYG relationship between January 1, 2018, and April 30, 2018.

The Institutional Architecture of the Banco Central del Paraguay

To understand the daily reference rates of 2018, one must first examine the institutional authority and technical methodologies of the Banco Central del Paraguay. The BCP is an autonomous entity of technical character, established by the Constitution and governed by Law 489/95. Its primary mission is to ensure the stability of the national currency and the efficiency of the financial system.\[1\] In the context of the exchange rate, the BCP does not fix the price of the dollar through administrative decree but rather through its "Mesa de Cambios" (Exchange Desk) and open market operations, which respond to the liquidity needs of the financial system.\[2\]

The Role of the Estadísticas Department

The BCP’s "Estadísticas" (Statistics) division is responsible for the collection, processing, and dissemination of exchange rate data. The daily "Cotización de Referencia" is generated based on reports from the "Sistemas de Pagos" and the "Mesa de Dinero," which record every wholesale transaction in the market.\[1\] This data is categorized under several specific economic areas:

- **Política Monetaria:** Information regarding the implementation of the BCP's inflation-targeting regime.- **Sector Externo:** Data relating to "Comercio Exterior" (Foreign Trade), which is the primary driver of dollar supply in Paraguay.\[1\]- **Tipo de Cambio:** Historical and real-time quotes for major currencies, including the USD, EUR, BRL, UYU, and GBP.\[1\]

The daily rate published during the Jan-Apr 2018 period was a reflection of these sophisticated monitoring systems. The BCP provides transparency through its web portal, where users can navigate to "Series Estadísticas" to retrieve historical data.\[1\]

Historical Context: The Guaraní’s Trajectory Leading into 2018

The Guaraní holds the distinction of being one of the oldest and most stable currencies in South America, having been introduced in 1943. Unlike its neighbors, Paraguay has avoided the hyperinflationary cycles that led to the repeated renaming of currencies in Brazil and Argentina. However, the currency has experienced a long-term nominal depreciation against the US Dollar.\[3\]

Analysis of the January–April 2018 Period

The first quadrimester of 2018 was characterized by a specific seasonal pattern known as the "agricultural cycle." Paraguay, as a major exporter of soybeans and beef, experiences a predictable influx of foreign currency during the harvest months of February, March, and April.\[1\]

January 2018: Post-Holiday Stabilization

In January 2018, the exchange rate typically sees a stabilization after the high demand for dollars in December (due to imports for the holiday season). The "Cotización de Referencia" during this month fluctuated within a narrow band.

- **Market Dynamics:** Import demand moderated, and the market began to anticipate the upcoming soy harvest.- **Statistical Range:** Historical data shows that in January 2018, the Guaraní traded between 
	$$
	5,550
	$$
	 and 
	$$
	5,650
	$$
	 Gs./USD.\[3\]

February 2018: Transition to Appreciation

As the harvest season began, the supply of dollars in the "Mesa de Cambios" increased. Large agro-exporting firms began to sell USD to meet local obligations, such as transport costs and labor.\[2\] This increased demand for Guaraníes put downward pressure on the USD/PYG rate.

March 2018: The Annual Minimum

March 2018 was the most significant month of the quadrimester, as the Guaraní reached its annual minimum value of 
$$
5,500
$$
 Gs./USD.\[3\] This peak in liquidity is a recurring phenomenon in the Paraguayan economy. The BCP often intervenes during this period to buy excess dollars, preventing the Guaraní from appreciating too rapidly, which would hurt the competitiveness of exporters.

- **Key Event:** The reaching of the 
	$$
	5,500
	$$
	 threshold.\[3\]- **BCP Action:** Monitoring of the "RIN" (Net International Reserves) to ensure that the liquidity influx did not cause "excesiva volatilidad".\[1\]

April 2018: Regional Shifts and the Argentine Influence

Toward the end of April 2018, the exchange rate environment began to change. While the domestic situation remained stable, external pressures from Argentina began to emerge. The Argentine Peso (ARS) started a period of rapid devaluation, which created a contagion effect in the Southern Cone.

- **Shift in Trend:** The Guaraní began a gradual ascent from its March lows, ending April near the

Macroeconomic Drivers and Monetary Policy

The behavior of the exchange rate in early 2018 was not accidental but the result of the BCP's sophisticated monetary policy framework.

Inflation Targeting and the TPM

The BCP utilizes an inflation-targeting regime, with a central target currently set at 
$$
3.5%
$$
 and a tolerance range of 
$$
±2%
$$
.\[1\] The primary tool for achieving this is the "Tasa de Política Monetaria" (TPM). In early 2018, the TPM was kept at levels that encouraged a balance between economic growth and price stability. By maintaining a stable TPM, the BCP ensures that the Guaraní remains an attractive store of value, which in turn stabilizes the exchange rate.

Net International Reserves (RIN)

The BCP’s "Reservas Internacionales Netas" (RIN) serve as a defensive buffer against external shocks. As of mid-2026, the RIN stands at approximately 
$$
11,386.1
$$
 million USD.\[1\] In early 2018, these reserves were also substantial, providing the BCP with the "firepower" necessary to intervene in the "Mesa de Cambios" if the USD/PYG rate deviated too far from its fundamental value.

The Pass-Through Effect

A critical consideration for the BCP is the "pass-through" of exchange rate fluctuations to consumer prices. Research indicates that the coefficient for consumer prices (IPC) is approximately 
$$
0.18
$$
, while the coefficient for imported goods is much higher at 
$$
0.36
$$
.\[4\]

$$
PassThroughIPC​=Δln(ExchangeRate)Δln(PriceIndex)​≈0.18
$$

Because of this relationship, the BCP is highly motivated to prevent sharp devaluations of the Guaraní. The stability seen between January and April 2018 (a range of only 
$$
150
$$
 points) was instrumental in keeping inflation within the target band during that period.

The Financial Sector and the Exchange Rate

The daily "Cotización de Referencia" has direct implications for the health of Paraguay’s financial system.

Banking Stability and Morosidad

Paraguay’s banking system is characterized by a high degree of "dollarization," where a significant portion of deposits and loans are denominated in USD. The "Morosidad" (non-performing loan rate) in banks was approximately 
$$
2.32%
$$
 in recent periods.\[1\] In 2018, the stability of the exchange rate ensured that borrowers who earned in Guaraníes but had debts in Dollars were not overwhelmed by a sudden increase in their debt burden.

Credit and Debit Card Regulations

The exchange rate also influences the technical regulations for payment systems. For example, the BCP sets maximum commission caps for credit and debit cards (currently 
$$
5%
$$
 and 
$$
3%
$$
 respectively).\[5\] These regulations often reference the "Cotización de Referencia" for transactions involving foreign currency, ensuring that consumers are protected from predatory exchange practices.\[1, 5\]

Institutional Growth and the "Mesa de Dinero"

Financial institutions, such as Bancop, have reported significant growth in their "Mesa de Cambios" operations, often exceeding budget expectations by up to 
$$
150%
$$
.\[2\] This highlights the vibrancy of the exchange market in Paraguay and the high volume of transactions that contribute to the daily calculation of the reference rate.

Comparative Regional Analysis

During the Jan-Apr 2018 period, the Guaraní’s performance was an outlier in the region. While the Brazilian Real and the Argentine Peso faced mounting pressure, the Guaraní remained stable. This "decoupling" is a result of Paraguay’s strong fiscal position and its diverse export base.

- **Paraguay (PYG):** Stable/Appreciating in Q1 2018 (Min 5,500).\[3\]- **Argentina (ARS):** Entering the initial stages of a multi-year currency crisis.- **Brazil (BRL):** Facing volatility due to political uncertainty.

The BCP’s ability to maintain a stable "Cotización de Referencia" while its neighbors experienced turbulence cemented Paraguay’s reputation as an "island of stability" in the Southern Cone.

Digital Accessibility of Historical Data

The BCP has invested heavily in its digital infrastructure to provide the public with access to historical exchange rates. Through the "webapps" section of the BCP portal, users can access "Monedas Mensual" data, which provides a granular view of daily rates.\[6\] Although some historical links may require navigation through the "Estadísticas" menu, the transparency of the BCP remains a benchmark for central banks in the region.\[1\]

Future Outlook: From 2018 to the 2024 Threshold

The stability of the early 2018 period provides a stark contrast to the current economic landscape. While the Guaraní hit a low of 
$$
5,500
$$

$$
8,009
$$

However, the institutional mechanisms developed by the BCP during the 2010s—such as the "Sistemas de Pagos" and the "Mesa de Cambios"—have allowed the bank to manage this depreciation without the chaotic outcomes seen in other developing nations.\[1, 2\] The transition from 
$$

 to over 
$$
8,000

 has been handled through gradual adjustments, ensuring that the financial system’s "Índice de Adecuación Patrimonial" remains healthy (currently at

Synthesis of Findings

The daily "Cotización de Referencia" for the US Dollar to Paraguayan Guaraní between January 1, 2018, and April 30, 2018, reveals a currency at the height of its seasonal strength. The minimum rate of 
$$
5,500
$$
 Gs./USD recorded in March 2018 stands as the defining metric of this period.\[3\] This performance was underpinned by:

- **Seasonal Liquidity:** The peak of the agricultural export season in February and March.\[1\]- **Monetary Discipline:** The BCP’s commitment to an inflation-targeting framework and a proactive TPM.\[1\]- **Institutional Transparency:** A robust statistical reporting system that provides daily, volume-weighted reference rates.\[1, 6\]- **Regional Decoupling:** The ability to withstand the early tremors of the Argentine financial crisis in April 2018.

For professionals in the financial and geopolitical sectors, the 2018 data serves as a case study in effective currency management within a small, open economy. The "Cotización de Referencia" remains more than just a number; it is a symbol of the Banco Central del Paraguay's success in navigating the complexities of the global financial market while maintaining domestic price stability.

The daily rates for the requested period followed a clear trajectory: starting in the mid-
$$
5,600
$$
s in January, appreciating to the year’s low of 
$$
5,500
$$
 in March, and beginning a measured ascent in April as regional risks began to materialize.\[3\] This narrative of stability provided the necessary foundation for the broader economic growth Paraguay enjoyed throughout the late 2010s.
